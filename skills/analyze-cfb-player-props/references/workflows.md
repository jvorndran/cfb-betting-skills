# Common Player-Prop Workflows

Use these as interchangeable modules rather than a required sequence.

## Analysis modules

### Identity and contract

Resolve the athlete, team, opponent, position, game, stat definition, period, threshold, price, and rules. A correct player joined to the wrong team or market is still a failed analysis.

### Role and availability

Determine whether the athlete is available and how the current rotation differs from season averages. Check teammates whose status changes the player's opportunity.

### Opportunity

Estimate team play volume and the player's relevant share: attempts, carries, targets, routes, snaps, red-zone work, returns, tackles, or another prop-specific unit. Do not substitute one measure for another.

### Efficiency

Estimate the rate that converts opportunity into the requested statistic. Separate repeatable efficiency from touchdowns, long plays, opponent mistakes, and small samples.

### Matchup and environment

Use opponent tendencies, scheme, pace, projected score states, venue, and weather when they have a causal relationship to opportunity or efficiency.

### Price and uncertainty

Compare low, base, and high role scenarios with the exact threshold and price. Identify the status, workload, or price trigger that would change the conclusion.

## Useful workflow shapes

- **Role-change analysis:** Define the game or personnel event that created the current role, then analyze only comparable post-change evidence.
- **Volume prop:** Center attempts, carries, targets, or receptions and treat efficiency as secondary.
- **Efficiency prop:** Start from expected opportunities, then examine yards, completions, or production per opportunity.
- **Touchdown prop:** Emphasize scoring opportunities and acknowledge the wide distribution of rare outcomes.
- **Portfolio comparison:** Apply consistent identity, price, and cutoff rules across several props while allowing different role models.
- **Retrospective:** Use only the role, quote, and information available before the historical game.

Stop when another source cannot alter the opportunity range, efficiency assumption, price threshold, or uncertainty classification.

# Common CFB Lines Workflows

These are modular patterns used by betting professionals. Combine, reorder, or omit them according to the market and evidence.

## Analysis modules

### Market definition

Verify the exact side, number or threshold, attached price, provider, time, and rules. Resolve different IDs and market conventions before comparing anything.

### Baseline

Estimate a broad expectation from team quality, opponent adjustment, venue, rest, and a cutoff-correct sample. Label prior-season information as a prior.

### Matchup

Investigate the mechanisms most likely to move the distribution: efficiency, success, explosiveness, pace, finishing drives, line play, havoc, field position, and scheme interactions. Avoid a generic checklist when only two or three dimensions matter.

### Personnel

Identify current quarterback, line, skill-position, defensive, and special-teams changes that affect the specific market. Separate confirmed facts from expected or speculative roles.

### Environment

Consider weather, surface, travel, rest, altitude, kickoff conditions, and likely game-state sensitivity only when they have a plausible path to the market.

### Price and sensitivity

Compare a defensible fair range with the exact market. Vary the assumptions that drive the answer and identify the threshold at which the conclusion changes.

## Useful workflow shapes

- **Market-first:** Start from a verified quote, identify what the market implies, then test the few assumptions most likely to be wrong.
- **Matchup-first:** Form a price-blind football range, freeze it, then compare it with the market.
- **Movement review:** Compare timestamped observations of the same market and distinguish number movement from price movement.
- **Scenario review:** Build a small set of plausible personnel, pace, weather, or score-state cases when one forecast would hide the real uncertainty.
- **Retrospective:** Use the price and information actually available at the historical cutoff; do not evaluate the decision using later outcomes.

Stop when additional evidence repeats an upstream data family or cannot change the price threshold, uncertainty, or conclusion.
